Many companies still run their contact center on their own infrastructure (on-premise). That model worked for years, but today it limits flexibility: it is hard to scale during peak seasons, it complicates remote work and it forces you to maintain and update servers on your own.

Moving the contact center to the cloud (a model known as CCaaS, Contact Center as a Service) solves many of those problems. This is a realistic guide to doing it well.

## Why migrate

- **Flexibility:** add or reduce agents according to demand, without buying equipment.
- **Remote or hybrid work:** agents can operate from anywhere.
- **Continuous updates:** new features arrive without costly upgrade projects.
- **Unified channels:** voice, chat, email and messaging on a single platform.

## Step by step

### 1. Understand your starting point

Document your volumes, channels, integrations (for example, with your management system or your CRM) and the processes that cannot fail. This initial snapshot defines the scope and prevents surprises.

### 2. Choose the right platform

Compare options based on your channels, your integrations and your expected growth. Platforms like Genesys Cloud offer intelligent routing, analytics and artificial intelligence, but what matters is that they fit your operation, not the longest feature list.

### 3. Define your data and integration strategy

Decide what information is migrated, what is archived and how the systems you will keep using are connected. A clean integration with your ERP or CRM is what prevents rework between areas.

### 4. Run a pilot before the full switch

Migrate one team or one channel first, measure and adjust. A pilot reduces risk and generates lessons before moving the entire operation.

### 5. Train agents and supervisors

Technology only pays off if the team masters it. Dedicate time to hands-on training and to supporting the first few weeks.

### 6. Make the switch and measure

Plan the transition to minimize interruptions and, once in operation, track the key metrics to confirm that the experience improves.

## Common mistakes worth avoiding

- Migrating everything at once without a pilot.
- Underestimating the integrations with existing systems.
- Leaving training until the end.
- Not defining how success will be measured before starting.

## A cost example (illustrative)

Costs vary a lot depending on the size and complexity of each operation. As a reference, a project usually includes three types of cost:

- **Platform subscription:** monthly, per agent.
- **Migration and integration:** a one-time cost at the start.
- **Training:** a one-off, at launch.

Against this, the savings come from no longer maintaining your own infrastructure and from operating more efficiently. It is worth running this calculation with real numbers from your operation; it is part of what we review in the assessment.

## What to ask your provider

- Open integrations with your business systems.
- Clear analytics and reports, not just scattered metrics.
- A migration plan with a pilot and hands-on support.
- Security and compliance appropriate to your industry.
