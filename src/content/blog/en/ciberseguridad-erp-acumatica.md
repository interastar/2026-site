If your operation handles contracts, payments and customer data, your enterprise resource planning (ERP) system is one of the most sensitive assets in the business. A security incident does not just interrupt operations: it can expose confidential information and damage your customers' trust.

The good news is that much of the risk can be reduced with solid practices and a properly maintained platform. Here is what you should expect from your ERP.

## Why ERPs are a target

An ERP concentrates valuable information: personal data, commercial terms, bank accounts and payment flows. Old or poorly maintained systems are especially vulnerable because they tend to go without security updates and rely on in-house infrastructure that is hard to monitor.

The most common risks are:

- **Ransomware:** malicious software that encrypts your information and demands a ransom.
- **Internal fraud or fraud through improper access:** people with more permissions than they need.
- **Data leaks:** sensitive information that leaves the organization.
- **Configuration errors:** doors left open through carelessness rather than a sophisticated attack.

## What a well-managed cloud ERP contributes

### Automatic updates and patches

One of the most frequent causes of incidents is running software that is not up to date. In a cloud ERP like Acumatica, security updates are applied centrally, without depending on someone installing them manually.

### Role-based access control

Each person should have only the permissions their job requires. Well-defined roles, together with multi-factor authentication (more than one way to verify identity when logging in), significantly reduce the risk of improper access.

### Encryption and backups

Information travels and is stored encrypted, and it is backed up automatically. This limits the damage from an incident and speeds up recovery.

### Audit log

Knowing who did what and when makes it possible to detect anomalous behavior and respond in time. A good audit log also makes regulatory compliance easier.

### Regulatory compliance

Depending on your country and industry, different data protection rules apply (in Mexico, for example, NOM-151 for the retention of data messages). A modern ERP makes it easier to comply with and document these requirements.

## The practical principle: "zero trust"

The *zero trust* approach (in English, **Zero Trust**) starts from a simple idea: do not assume something is secure just because it is inside the network. Every access is verified. In practice, this translates into minimal permissions, identity verification and constant monitoring, three things that a cloud platform makes easier.
